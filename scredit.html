“It is not virtue but the fear of punishment that keeps men within the bounds of duty.”

THOMAS HOBBES (1588-1679), English philosopher

Your task. Answer the 4 questions below on your own. Write at least 5 lines for each one (about 40 words or more).

Give one example from your own life in each answer, and use at least one connector of contrast (however, whereas, on the other hand).

Your answers are saved on this device while you type.

1In your own words, what does Hobbes mean? Do you agree with him?

Think of everyday life: school, the road, work, the internet.

2Think of a rule you follow. Do you follow it because you believe it is right, or because you are afraid of the consequences? What would you do if nobody could ever find out?

Be honest. A good answer gives a real example, not a general statement.

3Imagine a country where every citizen has a public score. It goes up when you behave well and down when you behave badly. Would people become more virtuous, or only more careful? Explain.

We will come back to this question at the end of the project.

4Should a government have the right to rate its citizens’ behaviour in order to keep order? Where would you draw the line?

Say what you would accept (for example CCTV, speed cameras, a bank credit score) and what you would refuse.

Now let’s see what happens in real life

In China, a system exists that is built on exactly this idea: the social credit system. Does it produce virtue, or only fear? Your job is to find out, and then to explain it to the world in one minute.
